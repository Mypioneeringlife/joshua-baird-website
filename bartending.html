People first

Hospitality first.
Everything else follows.

I spent roughly fifteen years behind bars, and what I learned is simple: great drinks matter, but people matter more. Every guest, regular or first-timer, deserves to feel welcome, seen, and taken care of.

A bar is a meeting place, a little reprieve from the noise, and hospitality is what turns it into home.

“It’s never just a drink. It’s a moment in someone’s day.”
The career arc

Milestones,
not mythology.

From a first private event in 2002 to World Class in 2019, the work changed in stages: learning the room, learning the business, discovering craft, competing, and eventually carrying the useful parts of that career into the rest of my life.

The craft

Intentional ingredients.
Better evenings.

Technique gives a drink structure, but intention gives it soul. The process became increasingly systematic: balance, aroma, dilution, texture, cost, service, and the question of what every ingredient was actually doing there.

Craft is not about showing off. It is about respecting the ingredients, the guest, and the experience.

“A great drink is a result of curiosity, not just a recipe.”
Before the name

There is the problem.

The whiteboard is where a lot of drinks really began. Flavor families, half-formed builds, things crossed out, things circled twice. Not every idea was supposed to survive. The point was to ask better questions until the drink had a reason to exist.

What I drink now

From cocktails
to mocktails.

Same standards. Different pour.

I spent years learning how alcohol behaves in a glass.

Whiskey brings oak, grain and warmth. Gin carries aromatics. Vermouth can contribute sweetness, herbs and acidity. Amari deliver bitterness. Liqueurs can bridge flavors that otherwise refuse to cooperate.

Those lessons were useful.

But eventually I realized something more important.

Alcohol was never the interesting part.

The interesting part was building balance.

It was discovering how acid changes sweetness. How salt can make fruit taste more like itself. How bitterness extends a finish. How aroma changes flavor before anything touches the tongue. How carbonation changes texture. How temperature changes perception. How a garnish can either complete a drink or become pointless decoration.

And above all, the interesting part was hospitality.

These days, I do not drink alcohol.

Given the choice, I favor the mocktail.

Not the old version of a mocktail where somebody removes the spirit, adds extra juice and charges nearly the same price. I mean drinks designed from the beginning to stand on their own.

The challenge is actually more interesting.

Without ethanol supplying heat, body and length, those characteristics have to come from somewhere else. Tea, spice, fermentation, shrubs, bitters, herbs, carbonation, tannin, smoke, fruit, vegetables and texture all become tools.

The same bartender brain is still there.

The ingredients changed.

And if fifteen years behind a bar taught me anything, it is that the glass was never really the point anyway.

“The person sitting across from it was.”
