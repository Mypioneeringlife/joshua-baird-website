Featured work

The pieces that earned a story.

01
Human / divided / together

Two people.
One canvas.

This is not primarily a painting about ethnicity. In this piece, the woman carries fire: passion, movement, instinct, intensity. The man carries calm: planning, structure, patience, organization. They are opposing energies, but not enemies. The tension comes from putting both forces on one canvas and asking what happens when neither one is allowed to erase the other.

02
The Crossroads

A blues story
in paint.

Robert Johnson was one of the defining voices of Delta blues: a singer, guitarist, and songwriter whose tiny recorded catalog became enormously influential. His 1936 recording “Cross Road Blues” helped turn the crossroads into one of the enduring images attached to his story, later tangled up with the famous legend that he traded his soul for musical ability. This painting is not an illustration of that legend. It takes the idea of the crossroads itself — choice, consequence, temptation, direction — and puts a golden pavilion at the center while the color and movement refuse to stay contained.

03
Destructive process

I damaged it
on purpose.

There was a period in my life when I was deliberately making destruction part of the process. This was not an accident, and it was not a drink getting spilled on a canvas. The painting was not drying the way I wanted, so I intentionally disrupted the surface with alcohol and heat and let the work scar, blister, and change. I was testing what would happen if I stopped treating the painting as something precious and started treating damage as another tool. The texture that survived became the point of the piece.
